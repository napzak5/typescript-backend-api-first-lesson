# AI Agent Guidelines

Instructions for AI coding agents that work in this repository, and the list
of the specialized agents defined for it.

## 📏 Rules

[CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) is the one statement of the project's conventions:
architecture, type-safety rules, naming, API–DB consistency, testing
requirements, the reference implementation and the definition of done. Every
agent reads it before changing anything.

This file and the agent definitions do not carry their own copy of those
conventions. If one of them seems to disagree with `CLAUDE.md`, `CLAUDE.md` is
right and the other file needs fixing.

The rules that most often decide whether a change is acceptable:

- No `any`, no type assertions (`as`), no type guards, no thrown exceptions in application code
- State and errors are sum types, handled exhaustively with ts-pattern; functions that can fail return a `Result`
- The API contract is written in TypeSpec first; types are generated, never written by hand
- The database schema is the source of truth: API property names are the column names, and a nullable column is a nullable property
- Nothing is built "for the future" (YAGNI); unused code is removed, not commented out

## 🧭 Working in the Repository

- **Toolchain**: the Node version in `.node-version`, and pnpm (the version in `packageManager` of `package.json`). Lint and format are Biome; tests are Vitest
- **What exists**: the Salon domain, the system endpoints, and every operation of the authentication contract: under `/api/v1/auth` login, refresh, logout, the current user, registration, email verification, password reset and change, verifying, accepting and joining the invitation of a staff member, two-factor authentication, and the list of the signed-in user's sessions with the revocation of one of them; under `/api/v1/admin/auth` the administrator operations (read a user, unlock an account, reset two-factor authentication). The Service domain exists as well: the service categories of a salon (`/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/service-categories`) and its services, with their options and packages (`/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/services`); see [docs/service-menu.md](docs/service-menu.md). Of the Customer domain the customer ledger exists: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/customers`, for administrators and the members of a salon; a signed-in customer reads, creates and updates their own record as a profile under `/api/v1/customers/me` and reaches no other customer; the preferences of a customer (the staff member they prefer and those they avoid, the times they like to come, how they like their hair and the service) are read and changed under `/api/v1/customers/{id}/preferences` by whoever keeps the ledger and under `/api/v1/customers/me/preferences` by the customer; the other operations of `CustomerCrud` have no backend code; see [docs/customers.md](docs/customers.md). Of the Staff domain the staff ledger of a salon exists: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/staff`, for administrators and the owner of that salon; so do the staff profiles, the part of the ledger that customers are shown: list and get under `/api/v1/staff-profiles`, for anyone, and get and update of a staff member's own profile under `/api/v1/staff-profiles/me`, for a signed-in staff member who reaches a salon (who writes the specialties, the biography, the photo, the years of experience and the certifications; the rest of the profile is the salon's to write, in the ledger); and so do the schedules of the staff, which say when a staff member works: list and update (the update replaces the whole schedule) under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/staff/{id}/schedules`, for administrators, the owner and the managers of that salon, and for a staff member to read their own; and so do the invitations by which a staff member gets a login account: get, create and delete under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/staff/{id}/invitation`, for administrators and the owner of that salon (only an administrator invites to the role of an owner), and verify, accept and join under `/api/v1/auth/staff-invitation`, for whoever holds the mailed token (to join, an account that exists signs in as well); and so do the accounts that are linked to the staff: get, update (the role) and delete (which unlinks) under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/staff/{id}/account`, for administrators and the owner of that salon (only an administrator gives the role of an owner); every operation of the staff contract has backend code; see [docs/staff.md](docs/staff.md). Every operation of the booking contract has backend code: the booking ledger under `/api/v1/bookings` (list, create, get, update, delete, the five actions on the status of a booking and reschedule) and the service lines of a booking under `/api/v1/bookings/{bookingId}/services` (add, get and remove), both for administrators and the members of the salon of a booking (owner, manager or staff; the delete of a booking not for the staff) and not for customer accounts, and the available slots of a salon under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/available-slots` (the list of what a customer can book for every signed-in user, the check of whether a staff member is occupied for those who keep the ledger), and a customer's own bookings under `/api/v1/customers/me/bookings` (list, create, get, cancel and reschedule, for a customer account with a profile: the customer books a slot that the list of available slots offers at that moment, reads their bookings at every salon without what the salon notes, cancels one before it begins, and moves one of a single service to another slot that the list offers for it, after which a confirmed booking is pending again); see [docs/bookings.md](docs/bookings.md). Of the payment contract the sales ledger exists: list, create, get, update and void of the sales of a salon under `/api/v1/sales`, for administrators and the members of the salon, the void for administrators, the owner and the managers (a sale is the bill of one visit as the till charged it; the server checks that its lines agree with themselves, that what it names exists and is of the salon, and that a booking is billed once, and compares no price, tax rate or commission with anything; an update holds the sale as it is afterwards to the same rules, and a void, which a payment that was not refunded stands in the way of, frees the booking for another sale); see [docs/sales.md](docs/sales.md). The payment methods of a salon exist as well: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/payment-methods` (what the salon accepts as payment: cash, cards, QR payments and the like; an administrator and the owner, the managers and the staff of the salon read them, an administrator, the owner and the managers set them up, and no customer account reaches them; the method of a payment method is fixed once it is created, its lower limit of the amount is never above the upper one, and a payment method that a payment refers to is deactivated instead of deleted, 409; a payment is held to whether its payment method is in use and to its limits); see [docs/payments.md](docs/payments.md). The payments of a sale exist as well: `POST /api/v1/sales/{id}/process` records a payment that was taken, for administrators and the members of the salon of the sale, and `POST /api/v1/payments/{id}/refund` refunds a completed payment once, in full or in part, for administrators, the owner and the managers (no payment processor is connected, and the payment status of a sale is counted from its payments in the transaction that writes one; see the same document). So do the receipt of a sale and its calculation: `GET /api/v1/sales/{id}/receipt` makes the receipt of a sale that is not void and received its total, again at every request and stored nowhere, under the sale number (409 otherwise), and `POST /api/v1/sales/calculate` tells what a sale would come to before it is entered, writing nothing: the lines of a booking at the amounts they copied, services added at the prices of the menu, the tax of the salon added once to their sum, and the kinds of payment method that take the total in one payment (see [docs/sales.md](docs/sales.md)). And the sales reports of a salon are implemented as well: the report of a period, the sales of a day, by staff member, by category, the trends and a CSV file of the days of a period under `/api/v1/sales-reports`, for an administrator and the owner and the managers of the salon (403 for the staff); they are counted from the sales that are not void at every request and stored nowhere, over a period of at most 366 days that the query states (see [docs/sales-reports.md](docs/sales-reports.md)). The points ledger of the customers is implemented too: the balance, the history and the points about to expire of a customer, and earning, using, adjusting and expiring points under `/api/v1/points`, one ledger for the whole platform; every movement writes a transaction with the balance after it, which the customer then has, and a movement that names a sale or a booking is refused for a void sale or a closed booking and is made once per record, and the expiry, an administrator's, writes one transaction per customer for what is left of the earnings past their expiry date (see [docs/points.md](docs/points.md)). The reviews of the customers are implemented too: the list, the get, the create, the change, the removal, the mark that a review was helpful, the report of one and the response of its salon under `/api/v1/reviews`, the list and the dismissal of the reports under `/api/v1/review-reports` and the summaries of a salon and of a staff member under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/reviews/summary` and `/api/v1/staff/{staffId}/reviews/summary`, read without signing in; a review is written and changed by the customer of a completed booking, once per booking, and removed by that customer or an administrator, and the transaction that writes, changes or removes it counts the rating and the review count of the salon again; a customer marks a review of another customer as helpful once, and its helpful count is the number of customers who did; a customer, or the owner or a manager of the salon, reports a review once to the administrators, who dismiss the report or remove the review, and its report count is the number of pending reports; the owner or a manager of the salon writes, replaces and takes down the response to a review, and an administrator takes one down (see [docs/reviews.md](docs/reviews.md)). Of the inventory contract the products of a salon are implemented: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/salons/{salonId}/products`, read by an administrator and the owner, the managers and the staff of the salon, kept by an administrator, the owner and the managers, and reached by no customer account; the three stock levels lie in order (`minimumStock` ≤ `reorderPoint` ≤ `maximumStock` among those that are set), which an update checks on the row as it would be, with the row held; the product code is unique among the products of the salon that are not deleted; and a delete marks the product deleted and is refused while it has stock (see [docs/products.md](docs/products.md)). The stock rows of a salon are implemented as well: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/inventory`, the lists of the low stock and of what expires soon, the stock movements of a row and their history, the stock count and the value report, read by an administrator and the owner, the managers and the staff of a salon, kept by an administrator, the owner and the managers, and reached by no customer account; a product kept by lots names the lot of each row and a product that is not has one row, which the repository decides with the product held; a row is created without stock and only its stock movements and the stock counts write the stock figures (a staff member records purchases, sales, uses and returns; an adjustment, a disposal, a transfer, a stock count and the value report are for an administrator, the owner and the managers); and a row that counts stock is not deleted (see [docs/inventory.md](docs/inventory.md)). The purchase orders of a salon are implemented as well: list, create, get, update, approve, cancel, receive and the reorder suggestions under `/api/v1/purchase-orders`; every member of a salon drafts an order of its stock rows and moves it on one step at a time, an administrator, the owner and the managers approve and cancel it, the items copy their products, the server works out the amounts, a member receives the goods of an ordered or shipped order once, recording a purchase of each item that arrived, and a delivered or cancelled order does not change (see [docs/purchase-orders.md](docs/purchase-orders.md)). The alerts of the stock of a salon are implemented as well: the list, the acknowledgement and the summary under `/api/v1/inventory-alerts`, for an administrator and the owner, the managers and the staff of a salon; an alert is not stored but worked out of the products, the stock rows and their movements whenever it is read (the stock of a product at or below its minimum or above its maximum, a stock row that expires within 30 days or has expired), and an acknowledgement records the alert as it is and holds while that lasts (see [docs/inventory-alerts.md](docs/inventory-alerts.md)). The treatment records of the bookings are implemented: list, create, get, update and delete under `/api/v1/treatments`, for an administrator and the owner, the managers and the staff of the salon of the booking (the staff do not remove one, and a customer account reaches none); a booking that is in progress or completed takes one record, whose customer, staff member, services and products are held to the booking and its salon, and recording the materials does not move the stock; the photos and the statistics are not served (see [docs/treatments.md](docs/treatments.md)). The other domains are specified in TypeSpec, some of them with tables in the database schema, but have no backend code. Follow the Salon files listed in `CLAUDE.md` when adding one; for data that belongs to a salon, read the service category files next to them, and the service files for what the categories do not have (a request that holds the rows of two tables, rules that tie two properties together, a bulk update, a soft delete). The customer files show data that belongs to no salon and is not public (flat routes, every operation authenticated, the permission decided from the actor alone before the request is read), and their `*-customer-profile` files data that belongs to the signed-in account (the route names no record, the account says which one). The customer preferences files show a record that every customer has, whether a row is stored or not (no create and no delete: a customer without a row has the empty preferences, and the first change creates the row; two routers that share their use-case code, mounted at their full paths, `/customers/me/preferences` before `/customers/:id/preferences`; IDs of another table that are looked up when they are written; a rule that ties two columns, checked on the row as it would be stored, in a transaction that locks the customer). The staff files show data that belongs to a salon and is not public (every operation authenticated, the permission decided from the salon of the path before the request is read, and a rule about the actor's own record whose refusal is a 409, not a 403). The staff profile files show a part of such data that everyone may read (a response model, a repository, use cases and a router of their own; only the shown columns are selected; no actor and no `authenticated` for the list and the get), and their `*-own-staff-profile` files the part of it that its owner writes (the route names no record and the actor says which one; a request with only the properties the owner writes, its schema picked from the one of the ledger; one `UPDATE` that has the condition of who is shown). The staff schedule files show rows that belong to a record of a salon and are replaced as a whole (a router mounted below the record, at `/salons/:salonId/staff/:id/schedules`; a `PUT` whose body is the array of all rows; a permission in two steps, for the salon and then for the record; a replacement in one transaction that locks the record). The staff invitation files show a record that a mailed token gives access to (one invitation per staff member, at `/salons/:salonId/staff/:id/invitation`, and three routes under `/auth/staff-invitation` for the holder of the token, two public and one that needs a session as well, because a mailed token is not a password; only the hash of the token is stored; a second permission that is decided from a value of the body; outcomes of the repository as sum types; writes that lock the staff member, and an account created by one statement against the unique index of the email). The staff account files show rows of another domain that a salon keeps (a part of `users`, at `/salons/:salonId/staff/:id/account`; a response model with what the salon is shown, and a repository that selects those columns only; an enum narrower than the one of its column, backed by a check constraint and listed in `narrowed` of its `check:api-db` rule; a rule about the actor's own record, a 409, checked after the body and before anything is looked up). The booking files show data of a salon whose routes are not below the salon (flat routes at `/bookings`; the salon comes from the body, the query or the stored row, so the permission is decided in two steps, from the actor alone before the request is read and for the salon once the request names it; what the actor does not reach is not found), a write whose rows are derived from the rows it refers to (`planBooking`, a pure function, called inside a transaction that locks them; no write mapper), and a rule between the rows of several bookings that no constraint can state (no staff member occupied beyond what they take at once: the staff members are locked `FOR UPDATE` in the order of their IDs, and the transaction states `READ COMMITTED`, so that a request that waited sees the booking it waited for)
- **Authentication and authorization**: the server checks the bearer token of every handler wrapped with `authenticated`, and the use case decides from the `Actor` whether the request is allowed. A new endpoint that is not public follows that pattern; see [docs/authentication.md](docs/authentication.md)
- **Mail**: the server sends the mails of the authentication flows over SMTP. Locally `make infra-up` starts Mailpit next to PostgreSQL, which keeps the mails and shows them at `http://localhost:8025`; see [docs/email.md](docs/email.md)
- **Generated files are never edited by hand**: `specs/tsp-output/`, `backend/packages/generated/src/`, `frontend/packages/api-client/src/generated/` (from `pnpm generate`), and `backend/packages/database/src/schema.ts`, `relations.ts`, `sql/setup.sql` (from `pnpm db:introspect` and `pnpm db:generate-sql`). They are committed together with the change that caused them
- **A migration that has been applied is never edited**: write a new file in `backend/packages/database/sql/migrations/`

Before reporting a task as done, run the checks CI runs:

```bash
make ci-check   # install, generated-file and API–DB checks, lint, format, build, typecheck, knip, unit and integration tests, audit
```

The integration tests need a PostgreSQL: the one at `TEST_DATABASE_URL` when
it is set, otherwise a container started through Docker. The mail delivery
tests always start a Mailpit container, so the full run needs Docker. When a change touches
the database schema, also run `pnpm check:db-schema` (it recreates the database
it is pointed at). Both `pnpm check:generated` and `pnpm check:db-schema`
compare the regenerated files with what git has, so they fail until
regenerated files are committed. If a check cannot be run, say so in the
report instead of reporting success.

## 🤖 Available Agents

The definitions are in `.claude/agents/`. The name is what a task is delegated to.

| Agent | Owns | Typical tasks |
|-------|------|---------------|
| [`salon-business-expert`](.claude/agents/salon-business-expert.md) | Business requirements | Salon operations and workflows, what a feature must do, validating a design against real practice |
| [`database-schema-architect`](.claude/agents/database-schema-architect.md) | `backend/packages/database` (schema, migrations, seeds) | Tables, constraints and indexes, SQL migrations, query performance |
| [`typespec-api-architect`](.claude/agents/typespec-api-architect.md) | `specs/`, and the API–DB consistency rules in `backend/packages/database` | Models and operations in TypeSpec, the OpenAPI document, type generation |
| [`backend-typescript-architect`](.claude/agents/backend-typescript-architect.md) | `backend/packages/{domain,infrastructure,api,config,utility}`, `backend/apps/server` | Use cases, repositories, mappers, handlers, middleware, backend tests |
| [`senior-frontend-architect`](.claude/agents/senior-frontend-architect.md) | `frontend/` | React components and applications, state, use of the generated API client, frontend tests |
| [`senior-ui-designer`](.claude/agents/senior-ui-designer.md) | Interface design | Layout, interaction, accessibility, the design system |
| [`design-review-architect`](.claude/agents/design-review-architect.md) | Cross-layer review | Agreement between database, API contract, domain code and UI; compliance with `CLAUDE.md` |
| [`documentation-specialist`](.claude/agents/documentation-specialist.md) | `docs/`, `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, this file, `.claude/agents/`, documents inside packages | Keeping the documentation true to the code |

## 🔀 Order of Work for a Feature

1. `salon-business-expert`: requirements and workflows
2. `database-schema-architect` and `typespec-api-architect`: the tables and the API contract, agreed together because property names, nullability and enums must match
3. Generation: `pnpm generate`, `pnpm db:introspect`, `pnpm db:generate-sql`, then `pnpm check:api-db`
4. `backend-typescript-architect`: domain, infrastructure and API layers with their tests
5. `senior-ui-designer` and `senior-frontend-architect`: the interface, on the generated client
6. `design-review-architect`: review across the layers
7. `documentation-specialist`: documentation that the change made untrue

The hand-over points and the checks at each of them are described in
[docs/multi-agent-collaboration-framework.md](docs/multi-agent-collaboration-framework.md).
